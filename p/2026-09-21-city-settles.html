First Light · 2026-09-21

a bedside lamp settling to its low warm setting at the wind-down chime, a whole city settling to its last warm windows at midnight, every one of them still burning

The lamp head dips as the chime clicks low. Brass cools against the wall. A rectangle of amber hardens on the floor, holding stillness. Just below it, a street glows through rain-slicked glass. Those ten squares dim one by one until only one remains. That last square swells, spilling outward past the curb. Buildings rise from the shrinking light like dark teeth. Thousands of rectangles flare in unison. The grid locks into place. Every window holds its fire while the frame tightens around it.

The image brief the local model wrote

A bold flat-colour screen-print poster, saturated and high-contrast, made of broad flat shapes with hard clean edges, every shape one solid colour cut like paper, with no shading, no gradient and no glow inside any shape, no photographic detail, no soft focus, no blur. Two colours own the whole image: a field of bright navy blue, and golden yellow for every lit thing and nothing else. Seen from inside the event, close: the event itself fills more than half of the image and runs out of the frame on every side, caught in the middle of happening, thrown across the picture on a diagonal, with streaks, spray, sparks or beams; one small figure at work, cut from flat paper as a plain navy silhouette with no face and no detail, is the only person. The whole image is one continuous scene from edge to edge. Behind the main form one wide flat area of unbroken navy rests the eye. Small things are crisp silhouettes. The subject: A grid of golden-yellow rectangles flares diagonally across the frame, their sharp edges slicing the navy field. A single brass lamp head dips, casting amber light across the floor. A small navy silhouette adjusts its base, hands shadowed, as storm-light spills through rain-slicked glass, illuminating the city’s burning windows.
