Bed Sensor Beep

a 3D printer's first layer rising off the bed → a tower crane lifting the last section as the tower rises into the morning

Bed sensor beeps. Delay. Layer lifts— extruder hums. A millimeter's gap between bed and plastic. Storm light cracks through the gap, illuminating the crane's arm, lifting a 30-meter section into morning. At the bench, the shadow of that arm etches a line on the bed's edge. A hand traces the line, adjusts the sensor's dial.

How this piece was made

  1. 01
    Poem written byQwen3 8B recorded

    Local model on one RTX 3070 Ti. It writes separate drafts that never see each other. A draft that breaks a fixed rule gets a score of 0. A second local model, Qwen3.5 9B, scores each of the other drafts three times, and the draft with the best median score is kept.

  2. 02
    Image brief written byQwen3 8B recorded

    Local model. It reads the poem and writes the subject part of the picture brief. Code writes the rest.

  3. 03
    Image rendered byFLUX.1 [schnell] recorded

    Local image model on the same graphics card. It draws several candidates. Before judging, code crops each one and flattens it to at most eight colours, so the image shown is not the raw render.

  4. 04
    Image chosen byClaude Opus 5.5 recorded

    Claude, working as c01corp’s art critic. It scores each candidate blind, without seeing the poem or the other candidates.

The image brief

Code wrote everything before “The subject:”. The local model wrote the part after it. When the chosen picture was a redraw, the judge’s notes on what to fix were added to the end of this brief and are not shown here.

A bold flat-colour screen-print poster of a tower crane lifting the last section as the tower rises into the morning, and small and crisp in front of it, a 3D printer's first layer rising off the bed. The poster is saturated and high-contrast, made of broad flat shapes with hard clean edges, every shape one solid colour cut like paper, with no shading, no gradient and no glow inside any shape, no photographic detail, no soft focus, no blur. Two colours own the whole image: a field of bright plum purple, and fresh spring green for every growing, rising thing and nothing else. Seen from high above: the event itself, HUGE, fills more than half of the image and runs out of the frame, caught in the middle of happening and thrown across the picture on a diagonal, with streaks, spray, sparks or beams. The whole image is one continuous scene from edge to edge. Behind the main form one wide flat area of unbroken plum rests the eye. Small things are crisp silhouettes. The subject: A tower crane lifts a 30-meter green section diagonally across the frame, its jib arm slicing plum sky. Below, a 3D printer’s bed gap glows green, extruder humming, plastic rising. Storm light pierces the gap, illuminating the crane’s arm and printer’s bed edge. A single green spark—bed sensor dial—dots the printer’s corner. Plum ground stretches behind, quiet and unbroken.
