First Light · 2026-09-17

a valve opened a quarter turn, water pouring, a dam's spillway gates open, the whole river pouring through

Hard noon bites the brass stem inside the wall. A quarter turn clicks free. Water screams from the spout, silver thread tearing into a heavy rope. It hits the porcelain, splashes upward against the glare, fills the sink with churning gray and rust-colored dust. Tiny leaves of debris dance in the white light, tumbling down the drain like shorn feathers. The room shakes with a low, wet thrum. Outside, the horizon cracks open without warning. Gates rise to let the mountain back in. A wall of brown water rises up to meet the sun, crushing the bridge span into splinters, carrying whole trees like matchsticks, swallowing the valley floor in one violent breath.

The image brief the local model wrote

A bold flat-colour screen-print poster, saturated and high-contrast, made of broad flat shapes with hard clean edges, every shape one solid colour cut like paper, with no shading, no gradient and no glow inside any shape, no photographic detail, no soft focus, no blur. Two colours own the whole image: a field of warm sand, ochre and rust, and cobalt blue for the moving water and nothing else. Seen from inside the event, close: the event itself fills more than half of the image and runs out of the frame on every side, caught in the middle of happening, thrown across the picture on a diagonal, with streaks, spray, sparks or beams; one small figure at work, cut from flat paper as a plain ochre silhouette with no face and no detail, is the only person. The whole image is one continuous scene from edge to edge. Behind the main form one wide flat area of unbroken ochre rests the eye. Small things are crisp silhouettes. The subject: A dam's spillway gates, fully open, pouring cobalt blue water diagonally across the frame, their metal edges slick with ochre rust; a brass valve stem, quarter-turned, glows cobalt blue at the base. Ochre sand fills the lower third, unbroken, as a small figure kneels beside the valve, hands flat, sunlight slants from the top-right, hitting the water’s crest.
