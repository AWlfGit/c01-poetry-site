Diode's First Breath

one LED on a workbench lighting for the first time → a whole harbour lighting up at dusk in one long curve of gold

Three counts, a click— the diode's glow starts at the edge, a breath before the light. The meter ticks, then the current flows— a single pinprick in the dark.

A mile of lights curves the harbor's edge, each bulb a heartbeat in the dusk's slow tide. Storm-light splits the curve—a single bulb fails, the gold spills uneven.

How this piece was made

  1. 01
    Poem written byQwen3 8B recorded

    Local model on one RTX 3070 Ti. It writes separate drafts that never see each other. A draft that breaks a fixed rule gets a score of 0. A second local model, Qwen3.5 9B, scores each of the other drafts three times, and the draft with the best median score is kept.

  2. 02
    Image brief written byQwen3 8B recorded

    Local model. It reads the poem and writes the subject part of the picture brief. Code writes the rest.

  3. 03
    Image rendered byFLUX.1 [schnell] recorded

    Local image model on the same graphics card. It draws several candidates. Before judging, code crops each one and flattens it to at most eight colours, so the image shown is not the raw render.

  4. 04
    Image chosen byClaude Opus 5.5 recorded

    Claude, working as c01corp’s art critic. It scores each candidate blind, without seeing the poem or the other candidates.

The image brief

Code wrote everything before “The subject:”. The local model wrote the part after it. When the chosen picture was a redraw, the judge’s notes on what to fix were added to the end of this brief and are not shown here.

A bold flat-colour screen-print poster of one LED on a workbench lighting for the first time, seen close and large, and far behind it, small, a whole harbour lighting up at dusk in one long curve of gold. The poster is saturated and high-contrast, made of broad flat shapes with hard clean edges, every shape one solid colour cut like paper, with no shading, no gradient and no glow inside any shape, no photographic detail, no soft focus, no blur. Two colours own the whole image: a field of bright navy blue, and golden yellow for every lit thing and nothing else. One small made thing fills the frame in extreme close-up, drawn as a big simple flat silhouette that runs out of the picture on more than half of the image, and the vast event is small and far behind it. The whole image is one continuous scene from edge to edge. Behind the main form one wide flat area of unbroken navy rests the eye. Small things are crisp silhouettes. The subject: A curved ribbon of golden lights arcs diagonally across the frame, its edges sharp and streaked with faint gold, illuminating the harbour’s edge. A single LED, tiny and crisp, glows gold at the curve’s start, casting its light over navy water. A vast navy field stretches behind the curve, unbroken, anchoring the scene. Storm-light fractures the gold, hitting the harbour’s curve.
