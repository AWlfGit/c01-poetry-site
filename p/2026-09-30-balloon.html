First Light · 2026-09-30

a kite lifting off a windy hill as the string pays out, one hot-air balloon lifting over the valley

Wind pulls the thin fiber from your fingers. The plastic tail snaps against the dry grass. String unspools, a white ribbon cutting the heat haze, carrying the blue diamond upward where the air turns sharp. It climbs on the slope's invisible ramp. A glider catches the updraft, wing broad and silent, then vanishes into a cloud that splits apart. Below, the valley floor tilts under a rising sphere, fabric billowing like a breath drawn deep and slow. The sun hangs low, casting long shadows across the tool chest. Back at the bench, the line trembles in its spool. Wood grain shifts as tension tightens the knot you just tied. Your thumb finds the slipknot fraying from the strain. You pull it free and begin to tie another.

The image brief the local model wrote

A bold flat-colour screen-print poster, saturated and high-contrast, made of broad flat shapes with hard clean edges, every shape one solid colour cut like paper, with no shading, no gradient and no glow inside any shape, no photographic detail, no soft focus, no blur. Two colours own the whole image: a field of saturated cerulean and sky blue, and scarlet red for every lifted thing and nothing else. Seen from inside the event, close: the event itself fills more than half of the image and runs out of the frame on every side, caught in the middle of happening, thrown across the picture on a diagonal, with streaks, spray, sparks or beams; one small figure at work, cut from flat paper as a plain cerulean silhouette with no face and no detail, is the only person. The whole image is one continuous scene from edge to edge. Behind the main form one wide flat area of unbroken cerulean rests the eye. Small things are crisp silhouettes. The subject: A scarlet red hot-air balloon rises diagonally, its rounded silhouette a continent-scale mass dwarfing the cerulean valley below, fabric billowing into a flat cerulean sky. A thin cerulean string snaps from the ground, its scarlet glow trailing upward. A small cerulean silhouette knots the string at mid-distance, backlit by late afternoon light casting long shadows across the cerulean field.
