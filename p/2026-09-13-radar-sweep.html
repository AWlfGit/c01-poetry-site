First Light · 2026-09-13

a bike trainer's pairing light sweeping from a blink to a steady beam as the phone finds it, an airport radar sweeping one great beam across the whole night sky

A small plastic ring on the bike stand hums, then the indicator snaps from red to white and holds it there. The phone camera hunts the glow until the signal locks, filling the dark porch room with a steady, unblinking eye. Outside, the late sun stretches shadows long across the concrete, turning the bicycle's rear wheel into a stationary shape against the ground.

A radar mast in the harbor cuts through the gray fog with a slow arc, painting invisible rings around the moored barges and silent buoys. No sound breaks the mist, just the mechanical sweep of its beam.

Now the runway light stretches one great fan across the night sky, a physical blade slicing the dark between the city blocks below. It catches the contrails left by a jet minutes ago, holding them suspended in the thin, high air while rotating again.
