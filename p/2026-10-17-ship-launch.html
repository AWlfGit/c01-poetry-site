Edge Lifts

a paper boat launched on a gutter stream → a ship launched, sliding down the slipway into the harbour in a wall of spray

Edge lifts. A splash. Two seconds later, the hull cracks the surface. The current takes it—tiny ripple, then gone. A paper fold snaps into the stream's throat, where the gutter bends. The water holds it for a breath, then spits it out. The boat's edge glints, a shard of white in the long-shadow afternoon.

Spray soaks the dock's steel rail—ten meters of white. The ship slides down the slipway, wall of spray. The harbor echoes with the roar of water, a sound like a hammer striking a hull. The slipway's angle, forty-five degrees, carves the ship's path into the sea.

A wave etches the bench's edge, a line where the spray reached. A hand folds another boat from the same sheet.

How this piece was made

  1. 01
    Poem written byQwen3 8B recorded

    Local model on one RTX 3070 Ti. It writes separate drafts that never see each other. A draft that breaks a fixed rule gets a score of 0. A second local model, Qwen3.5 9B, scores each of the other drafts three times, and the draft with the best median score is kept.

  2. 02
    Image brief written byQwen3 8B recorded

    Local model. It reads the poem and writes the subject part of the picture brief. Code writes the rest.

  3. 03
    Image rendered byFLUX.1 [schnell] recorded

    Local image model on the same graphics card. It draws several candidates. Before judging, code crops each one and flattens it to at most eight colours, so the image shown is not the raw render.

  4. 04
    Image chosen byClaude Opus 5.5 recorded

    Claude, working as c01corp’s art critic. It scores each candidate blind, without seeing the poem or the other candidates.

The image brief

Code wrote everything before “The subject:”. The local model wrote the part after it. When the chosen picture was a redraw, the judge’s notes on what to fix were added to the end of this brief and are not shown here.

A bold flat-colour screen-print poster of a ship launched, sliding down the slipway into the harbour in a wall of spray, and small and crisp in front of it, a paper boat launched on a gutter stream. The poster is saturated and high-contrast, made of broad flat shapes with hard clean edges, every shape one solid colour cut like paper, with no shading, no gradient and no glow inside any shape, no photographic detail, no soft focus, no blur. Two colours own the whole image: a field of pale sand and yellow ochre, and cobalt blue for the moving water and nothing else. Seen from inside the event, close: the event itself fills more than half of the image and runs out of the frame on every side, caught in the middle of happening, thrown across the picture on a diagonal, with streaks, spray, sparks or beams; one small figure at work, cut from flat paper as a plain ochre silhouette with no face and no detail, is the only person. The whole image is one continuous scene from edge to edge. Behind the main form one wide flat area of unbroken ochre rests the eye. Small things are crisp silhouettes. The subject: A cobalt-blue ship slides diagonally down a steel slipway, its hull a jagged silhouette of a continent, dwarfing the paper boat—a folded white shard—flickering in the gutter’s bend. Ochre sand stretches behind the spray-wall, long shadows stretching from upper-right light. A flat ochre figure folds another boat from the same sheet, hands mere pixels, as waves etch the dock’s edge.
