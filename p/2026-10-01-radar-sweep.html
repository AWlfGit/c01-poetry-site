First Light · 2026-10-01

a bike trainer's pairing light sweeping from a blink to a steady beam as the phone finds it, an airport radar sweeping one great beam across the whole night sky

The trainer light snaps on then steadies, a green dot finding the phone screen. It spins once in the corner where the window throws its square of white floor. The beam cuts dust motes into tiny stars that orbit the plastic hub. A harbor mast outside turns slow against the fogged glass. Then the airport radar wakes up high above the city grid, and its invisible wall sweeps through clouds and dark water alike. The sweep is a single hand erasing everything but the air it passes. Back down to the bench, the small green circle keeps turning. It catches the heel of my shoe where it stands fixed on the rubber pad. The beam slides under my toes again as I lift my foot to step.

The image brief the local model wrote

A bold flat-colour screen-print poster, saturated and high-contrast, made of broad flat shapes with hard clean edges, every shape one solid colour cut like paper, with no shading, no gradient and no glow inside any shape, no photographic detail, no soft focus, no blur. Two colours own the whole image: a field of rich violet and indigo, and electric cyan for every signal, beam and glow and nothing else. Seen close: the event itself, HUGE, fills more than half of the image and runs out of the frame, caught in the middle of happening and thrown across the whole picture on a diagonal, with streaks, spray, sparks or beams. The whole image is one continuous scene from edge to edge. Behind the main form one wide flat area of unbroken violet rests the eye. Small things are crisp silhouettes. The subject: A sweeping electric cyan beam arcs diagonally across the frame, its edge a continent-like silhouette dwarfing the violet sky and ground. A small crisp cyan dot glows at the beam’s start, where it meets the violet floor. The beam’s motion cuts dust motes into tiny stars, its force erasing all but the air it passes, lit by the radar’s invisible wall. The bike trainer’s light pulses briefly before merging into the beam’s steady glow.
