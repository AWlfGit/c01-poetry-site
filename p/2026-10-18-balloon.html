String Tension

a kite lifting off a windy hill as the string pays out → one hot-air balloon lifting over the valley

String taut, then slack—each meter unwound as wind hums through the spool's thin groove. The kite's wing catches, a flicker of lift, then holds—no tug, no flutter, just weight released. The hill's slope softens, the string drags slower, a measurement in air.

Balloon ascends, past the smokestack's height— 120 meters—its fabric tight against the valley's breath. Trees bend as it swells, a bubble of warmth piercing the dark. Windows flare below, their light a grid of fixed stars.

The bench's edge now bears a smear of ash, smudged where the wind spun its own thread. A hand ties a knot in the string's leftover twist.

How this piece was made

  1. 01
    Poem written byQwen3 8B recorded

    Local model on one RTX 3070 Ti. It writes separate drafts that never see each other. A draft that breaks a fixed rule gets a score of 0. A second local model, Qwen3.5 9B, scores each of the other drafts three times, and the draft with the best median score is kept.

  2. 02
    Image brief written byQwen3 8B recorded

    Local model. It reads the poem and writes the subject part of the picture brief. Code writes the rest.

  3. 03
    Image rendered byFLUX.1 [schnell] recorded

    Local image model on the same graphics card. It draws several candidates. Before judging, code crops each one and flattens it to at most eight colours, so the image shown is not the raw render.

  4. 04
    Image chosen byClaude Opus 5.5 recorded

    Claude, working as c01corp’s art critic. It scores each candidate blind, without seeing the poem or the other candidates.

The image brief

Code wrote everything before “The subject:”. The local model wrote the part after it. When the chosen picture was a redraw, the judge’s notes on what to fix were added to the end of this brief and are not shown here.

A bold flat-colour screen-print poster of one hot-air balloon lifting over the valley, and small and crisp in front of it, a kite lifting off a windy hill as the string pays out. The poster is saturated and high-contrast, made of broad flat shapes with hard clean edges, every shape one solid colour cut like paper, with no shading, no gradient and no glow inside any shape, no photographic detail, no soft focus, no blur. Two colours own the whole image: a field of saturated cerulean and sky blue, and scarlet red for every lifted thing and nothing else. Seen close: the event itself, HUGE, fills more than half of the image and runs out of the frame, caught in the middle of happening and thrown across the whole picture on a diagonal, with streaks, spray, sparks or beams. The whole image is one continuous scene from edge to edge. Behind the main form one wide flat area of unbroken cerulean rests the eye. Small things are crisp silhouettes. The subject: A hot-air balloon, scarlet red, balloons upward diagonally across the frame, its fabric tight and taut, swelling like a bubble of warmth against cerulean sky. A kite, string coiled in cerulean, drags behind, its wing catching wind. Lit windows below cast a grid of white sparks on cerulean hills. The balloon’s ascent fills the frame, streaked with red, while the hill’s edge bears a smear of ash. No figures, no detail—just the event and its force.
