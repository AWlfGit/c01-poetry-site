First Light · 2026-09-22

a kettle boiling on a camp stove, a geyser boiling over in one great column of steam

Hard noon light hits the camp stove plate. A kettle sits there, copper rim hot. Water rattles against the metal bottom. Bubbles break and pop with wet sounds. Steam rises in a thin gray curl. The lid shudders on its bent spring. Tiny explosions burst from the spout. Then earth opens wide beneath the bench. A geyser column shoots straight to the sky. Great clouds of white stuff fill the frame. Steam knocks the air off the ground. It boils like a mountain made of breath. The small kettle on the stove keeps doing its thing. Water inside jumps high above the ring. Hand reaches forward with tongs.

The image brief the local model wrote

A bold flat-colour screen-print poster, saturated and high-contrast, made of broad flat shapes with hard clean edges, every shape one solid colour cut like paper, with no shading, no gradient and no glow inside any shape, no photographic detail, no soft focus, no blur. Two colours own the whole image: a field of bright teal blue-green, and orange-red for every burning, glowing thing and nothing else. Seen close: the event itself, HUGE, fills more than half of the image and runs out of the frame, caught in the middle of happening and thrown across the whole picture on a diagonal, with streaks, spray, sparks or beams. The whole image is one continuous scene from edge to edge. Behind the main form one wide flat area of unbroken teal rests the eye. Small things are crisp silhouettes. The subject: A vertical column of orange-red steam erupts diagonally across the frame, billowing upward in a jagged, solid mass of white-hot vapor. Below, a small copper kettle sits on a teal stove plate, its lid trembling as orange-red bubbles burst. Teal sky and ground form a quiet field around the steam’s base, lit by hard noon light striking the kettle’s rim and the rising column’s flat, glowing core.
