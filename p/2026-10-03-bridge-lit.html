First Light · 2026-10-03

a lantern lighting up on a pier post, the whole bridge lit up, one great arc of gold across the strait

Storm-light cuts a gap in the dark sky. A lantern blooms on a wooden pier post, wax melting slow against the rain. It feeds the water below with liquid gold. Then sudden heat spreads through the steel ribs, cable by cable, span by span, until one great arc of fire crosses the strait. The whole frame fills with it now. Heavy cables hum and hold their tension tight, glowing solid against the blackening sea. This heavy bridge hangs low in the night's wind, a physical thing lit from end to end, holding its shape mid-motion over the water.

The image brief

Code wrote everything before “The subject:”. The local model wrote the part after it. When the chosen picture was a redraw, the judge’s notes on what to fix were added to the end of this brief and are not shown here.

A bold flat-colour screen-print poster, saturated and high-contrast, made of broad flat shapes with hard clean edges, every shape one solid colour cut like paper, with no shading, no gradient and no glow inside any shape, no photographic detail, no soft focus, no blur. Two colours own the whole image: a field of bright navy blue, and golden yellow for every lit thing and nothing else. Seen from high above: the event itself, HUGE, fills more than half of the image and runs out of the frame, caught in the middle of happening and thrown across the picture on a diagonal, with streaks, spray, sparks or beams. The whole image is one continuous scene from edge to edge. Behind the main form one wide flat area of unbroken navy rests the eye. Small things are crisp silhouettes. The subject: A golden arc of light spans the strait, a colossal bridge silhouette cut like paper, its steel ribs glowing solid yellow against navy. A tiny lantern blooms on a wooden pier post, wax melting slow, feeding the water below with liquid gold. Storm-light cuts a gap, illuminating the cables’ tension as they hum, holding the frame’s weight in a diagonal sweep across the darkened sea.
