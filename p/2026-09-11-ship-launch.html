First Light · 2026-09-11

a paper boat launched on a gutter stream, a ship launched, sliding down the slipway into the harbour in a wall of spray

A folded square of newsprint slips the curb's edge and slides on wet asphalt toward the drain. It gathers three drops of rain into a belly before pitching forward, nose down, hunting the dark water in a narrow throat. The paper flutters once, then rights itself on a current that tastes of oil and dust, carrying the folded corner like a sail up the slight grade where leaves float sideways.

A steel beast heaves its bow at the slipway's end and slides without braking into the deep harbor gray. Water explodes in a single white wall that drowns the dockworkers standing behind the rail, filling the frame with salt and roaring force. The hull cuts a clean line through the black tide, leaving only foam drifting against the piers.

Back to the curb where the paper boat still glides across a puddle reflecting fractured sky light, pushed by a breeze that smells of coming rain. The folded edge touches a stone on the bench and lifts again, never slowing, still moving forward as the storm opens wide.
